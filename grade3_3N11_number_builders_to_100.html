2. Write Number Words as Numerals

Worked Example

seventy-two
“seventy” means 7 tens = 70.
“two” means 2 ones.
Put the parts together: 70 + 2 = 72.
Quick check: Read 72 aloud. It says “seventy-two.”

Your Turn

Common mistake: Do not squeeze out a zero that holds an empty place. For 100, write all three digits: 100 means 1 hundred, 0 tens, and 0 ones.

3. Build Numbers with Place-Value Pieces

Botley the Base-Ten Robot says:
“Ten of one unit can be regrouped as one unit in the next place. That is why 10 tens = 1 hundred.”

Why regrouping works

80 = 8 tens = 80 ones

Both descriptions have the same value. We changed the groups, not the amount.

100 = 10 tens = 1 hundred

Ten tens can be traded for 1 hundred, so the total is still 100.

4. Same Number, Different Groups

Botley the Base-Ten Robot says:
“A strong number builder can prove two descriptions are equal by trading 10 smaller units for 1 larger unit.”

Worked Example: 64

Start: 6 tens + 4 ones.
Trade 1 ten for 10 ones.
Now we have 5 tens + 14 ones.
Check: 50 + 14 = 64. Same value!

Find the missing group

Strategy: First find the value of the groups you already know. Then ask what value is left. Because 10 ones make 1 ten and 10 tens make 1 hundred, you can trade without changing the total.
